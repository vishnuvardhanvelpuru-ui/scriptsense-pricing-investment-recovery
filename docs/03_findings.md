# Management findings and pricing recommendation

Fictional case, simulated customers, INR. Results are modelled, not achieved. Snapshot: 31 August 2026.

## Current economics
36 enterprise customers generate ₹9.53 lakh per month (₹1.143 crore annualized). Direct service cost is ₹3.46 lakh; gross profit is ₹6.07 lakh and gross margin 63.70%. Including ₹5 lakh fixed overhead, total cost is ₹8.46 lakh and monthly operating surplus is ₹1.07 lakh. Average annualized contract value is ₹3.1755 lakh.

| Segment | Customers | Revenue ₹ lakh/month | Gross profit ₹ lakh/month | Gross margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Independent Studios | 11 | 2.36 | 1.68 | 71.01% |
| Production Houses | 14 | 3.63 | 2.43 | 66.98% |
| Streaming Studios | 11 | 3.54 | 1.96 | 55.47% |

Production Houses and Streaming Studios contribute more total gross profit than Independent Studios in this dataset; judge the rate as well as the amount in the table. This reflects the synthetic mix, not a market-level claim about which buyers will pay more. Enterprise tier contributes ₹3.649 lakh/month before overhead and 57.47% of revenue from one-third of customers.

## Weak contracts and revenue exposure
The largest five customers supply 25.72% of revenue. Loss of a large/high-margin customer will not behave like a uniform retention haircut. Account C012 loses ₹5,000/month before overhead: ₹9,600 fee versus ₹14,600 service cost. C024 earns only ₹400/month before overhead. High support usage explains these weak contracts; a small blanket increase is not a complete fix.

There are 17 contracts requiring review: 6 primarily for margin, 7 for usage, and 4 for discount. These are mutually exclusive priority reasons. Nine contracts have a 20% discount overall, even where a higher-priority margin/usage flag is assigned.

## Value-oriented tiered proposal
Core: ₹12,960 list base with 25 monthly script runs and ₹200/run overage. Target smaller studio teams doing initial script screening. Growth: ₹27,500 with 80 runs and ₹150/run overage, positioned for repeated slate comparisons and team review. Enterprise: ₹56,000 with 250 runs and ₹100/run overage, positioned for larger slates and deployment needs. Team workflow and service entitlements must be validated before a real offer; no unverified capability or SLA is promised.

The value metric is script-analysis volume and decision workflow, rather than simply a markup on hosting. The overage charge creates a usage boundary; bulk buyers receive a lower marginal rate. These precise prices are pilot hypotheses, not externally benchmarked or empirically optimized prices. Retain existing base discounts in the model to avoid assuming instant renegotiation success. Review discount renewal criteria separately instead of counting its benefit twice.

At full adoption and retention, the base-fee changes add ₹1.03587 lakh/month. Usage overages add ₹0.25150 lakh/month. Total gross price opportunity is ₹1.28737 lakh/month before loss of customers. Charge only under accepted new renewal terms.

## Pricing and recovery scenarios
Conservative: 50% adoption / 98% retention. Base: 100% adoption / 95% retention. Upside: 100% adoption / 100% retention. No customer growth or cost reductions are invented.

| Scenario | Revenue ₹ lakh/month | Gross profit ₹ lakh/month | Gross margin | Surplus ₹ lakh/month | Payback years |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current | 9.53 | 6.07 | 63.70% | 1.07 | 19.50 |
| Conservative | 9.97 | 6.58 | 66.00% | 1.58 | 13.20 |
| Base | 10.27 | 6.99 | 68.02% | 1.99 | 10.48 |
| Upside | 10.81 | 7.36 | 68.02% | 2.36 | 8.84 |

The Base case adds ₹0.74668 lakh revenue and ₹0.91958 lakh gross profit per month after the retention haircut. Gross margin changes by 4.32 percentage points. Operating surplus improves by the same profit amount because fixed overhead is unchanged.

## ₹2.5 crore cannot be recovered quickly through this repricing alone
Current payback is 19.50 years, Base 10.48 and Upside 8.84. None meets the illustrative 60-month benchmark. Five-year recovery needs ₹4.1667 lakh monthly surplus; Upside produces ₹2.35587 lakh. The required retention threshold is 124.62%, above 100%, so that target is infeasible under these prices and unchanged scale. Customer growth, more valuable offerings or lower operating costs would also be necessary, but are not quantified without evidence.

## Management actions
1. Pilot accepted renewal terms with accounts above proposed usage allowances. Measure realized price, acceptance, retained revenue and service cost before rollout.
2. Review C012 and C024 support delivery and scope first. Consider paid service packages or support boundaries, subject to the customer's needs and agreed terms. Do not assume termination is automatically best.
3. Use segment and tier gross profit to prioritize discovery interviews. Test the value of faster script screening and slate decisions; do not infer willingness to pay from hosting cost alone.
4. Monitor concentration and named account losses. Do not treat 95% uniform retention as an observed forecast.
5. Manage the investment objective separately from revenue targets. Keep full overhead, cash timing and deployment costs visible. Re-estimate after the pilot; avoid promising seed-investment recovery from invoiced revenue.

If support payroll remains fixed after churn, Base payback is longer. The workbook includes that sensitivity and a 10% overhead stress. These tests matter more than claiming a precise long-range forecast from fictional rows.
